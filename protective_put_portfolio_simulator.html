Model, assumptions & data provenance

Funding: the equity allocation is the equity share of funds left after paying the premium; the rest is cash. In coverage mode, if equity target is w, put notional coverage h and modeled premium per unit of spot p, initial equity weight is w/(1+w·h·p), put notional is h times equity, premium is notional × p, and cash is the residual. In budget mode, a fixed fraction of beginning capital is spent on options and the remaining capital is split between equity and cash. No borrowing; fractional index/option units.

Pricing: European Black–Scholes with S=1, K=1−OTM, T=1, annualized volatility, prior December US 1-year Treasury yield (converted to continuously compounded rate), and prior December S&P trailing dividend yield. Historical mode uses the previous year-end VIX (30-day annualized implied volatility) plus a configurable additive IV adjustment (default +3 percentage points). VXO, based on the S&P 100, is used for purchases 1987–1990 to initialize early rolling averages. The adjustment is an assumption, not a calibrated historical 1-year volatility surface. Premium markup approximates execution friction. Actual premiums depend on the term structure, skew, dates, spreads and contract specifications. The 1-year put is an idealized annual European cash-settled contract.

Settlement: max(0, K−ST) × put notional uses the USD price return, not total return. Equity earns actual total return including reinvested dividends. Option premiums are paid upfront and expired or settled once per year, with no intrayear monetization, rolling or trading. Cash earns the selected yield; historical USD cash uses the prior December average 1-year Treasury yield as a held-to-maturity return proxy. No taxes, fees beyond configurable option markup, or inflation. EUR reports translate the USD equity, options and USD cash at annual FX factors from the preceding simulator (ECU proxy before 1999); fixed investor-currency cash is not translated. Annual drawdown uses year-end values only; real intrayear loss can be materially larger. Put coverage does not hedge exchange-rate risk.

Sources: Slickcharts S&P 500 price and total returns (more precise than earlier rounded simulator), History of Market year-end VIX / FRED VIX, FRED VXO, FRED GS1, Multpl historical dividend yield.
